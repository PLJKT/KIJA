# KIJA (PT Kawasan Industri Jababeka Tbk) — Financial & Operations Analysis

**Period covered:** FY2015–FY2025, plus 1H2026 interim (unaudited)
**Reporting currency:** IDR billion unless stated
**Data as of:** 18 Sep 2026; share price IDR 180

---

## 1. Business model

KIJA is an integrated industrial-estate and township developer operating two core clusters:

- **Cikarang (Jababeka, Bekasi)** — the original 30+ year-old integrated city: industrial land, residential, commercial, dry port, power (Bekasi Power), water.
- **Kendal Industrial Park (Central Java)** — a 51%-owned JV with Sembcorp, SEZ status, the current main growth engine.

Revenue splits into (a) **land and property sales** (lumpy, cyclical) and (b) **recurring infrastructure** (power, water, dry port, service fees). The mix has shifted from ~45% recurring in 2021 to ~57% in 2025, which is the key quality improvement.

## 3. Key trends

### 3.1 Revenue and profit
- Revenue troughed at IDR 2,254bn in 2019, then doubled to 5,149bn by 2025 — driven by Kendal land sales and infrastructure ramp.
- Consolidated net profit recovered from a 45bn trough (2020) to 857bn (2025).
- **Parent net profit is structurally lower (~49% of consolidated)** because the Kendal JV is 51% KIJA / 49% Sembcorp; NCI takes roughly half.

### 3.2 Margins
- Gross margin: 44% (2015) → peak 52% (2022) → 39% (2025) → 34% (1H26). Compression reflects a higher mix of lower-margin land recognition and infrastructure (power/water) vs. high-margin land sales.
- EBITDA margin: stable ~33–38% throughout, indicating recurring-infrastructure earnings are resilient.
- Net margin: recovered from ~1% trough (2018–22) to 16.6% (2025).

### 3.3 Cash flow
- CFO inflected sharply from ~500bn/yr (2015–22) to 1,870bn (2024) and 2,402bn (2025).
- Conservative FCF (after land acquisition) was negative every year 2015–2018 (heavy land banking), turned positive in 2019, and reached 1,738bn (33.7% of revenue) in 2025.

### 3.4 Balance sheet and debt
- Net debt/EBITDA: 2.7x (2023) → 0.6x (2025); cash 3.6tn vs total debt 4.6tn.
- Q2 2026: 15-year IDR 4.6tn Mandiri facility repaid the 2027 USD senior notes; FX risk largely eliminated.
- Moody's assigned first-time rating B3 (Jan 2026); S&P B-.

## 4. Risks

1. **Margin compression** — gross margin down from 52% peak to 34% (1H26); structural if land sales mix stays low.
2. **Minority dilution** — parent takes only ~49% of consolidated profit; Sembcorp's 49% Kendal stake is a permanent drag on per-share earnings.
3. **Revenue timing** — 1H26 revenue −11% y/y; land recognition drives ±20% quarterly swings.
4. **Kendal concentration** — ~70% of marketing sales from one estate; Central-Java execution and tenant demand are key.
5. **Working capital** — DSO rose from 37d (2025) to 54d (1H26); receivables 721bn vs 521bn.

---

## 5. Top 3 improvement priorities

1. **Lift parent share of profit** — buy up Sembcorp's Kendal stake or increase JV dividends. If parent share rose to 60%, FY25 attributable profit would be ~514 vs 423 (+~91bn, +21%).
2. **Shift mix to recurring infrastructure** — push power/water/dry-port volumes to ~60% of revenue; blended GPM could rise 2–3pct.
3. **Smooth land-sale timing** — convert more sales to take-or-pay / recurring service contracts; grow pre-sales backlog.

## 6. Market opportunities (2026)

- **Cikarang reshoring**: SEA's largest industrial estate; land prices ~IDR 3.6m/sqm, +4% vs end-2025 (CBRE Q2-2026). EV-battery / nickel downstreaming is the largest inbound FDI.
- **Kendal SEZ momentum**: three new investor MoUs signed Sept 2026 (synthetic leather, garment, hospitality); 144 tenants as of Aug 2026.
- **2026 sales target**: IDR 3,750bn marketing sales (Kendal 2,500 / Cikarang 1,250); 1Q26 realised 541bn.
- **New products**: Jababeka Bizpark phase-I handed over (won Indonesia Property Award 2026).
- **Balance sheet capacity**: near net-cash; ability to fund land banking or buy out JV partners.
